## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The site has a strong monochrome identity, focused positioning, real screenshots, and good copy. The weakest tell is repeated generated-looking composition: tiny uppercase mono labels above most sections, interchangeable large rounded cards, and excessive section uniformity. The result is polished but sometimes feels assembled from a marketing template rather than authored around Unit's notebook-like product character.

**Deterministic scan:** The detector reported font-size advisories across compare, program, updates, header, footer, FAQ, showcase, and global type utilities. Most hero/display values are intentional marketing tokens, while narrative 15px copy and 10–13px non-metadata copy are actionable. No prohibited color, spacing, or component violations were found.

**Visual overlays:** Browser mutation is unavailable in the read-only browser inspection path, so no reliable overlay was injected. Desktop and 390px mobile screenshots plus DOM inspection were used as the fallback signal.

## Overall Impression

Unit already looks confident and product-led. The single biggest opportunity is to replace the repetitive card-and-eyebrow grammar with editorial product rows, stronger type rhythm, and fewer decorative containers.

## What's Working

- The hero promise is immediate: the user understands progressive overload and the next-target benefit before scrolling.
- Real app screenshots and human photography establish trust without generic fitness-dashboard imagery.
- The monochrome palette, tokenized spacing, visible focus styles, reduced-motion support, and semantic headings provide a strong system foundation.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Marketing photos can remain visually stuck as placeholders

- **Why it matters:** A hero photograph that appears missing makes the live page look broken and undermines trust at the highest-attention moment.
- **Fix:** Make the real local asset visible by default, reserve the placeholder for actual failures, and use `onError` rather than an `onLoad` opacity gate that races cached images.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

### [P2] Repeated card grids flatten the product story

- **Why it matters:** Four showcase cards and six secondary-feature cards receive equal weight, forcing users to parse everything and making the page feel template-generated.
- **Fix:** Convert the main showcase to alternating editorial rows and the supporting feature set to compact divided rows with contained visuals.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] Eyebrow labels are used as a default scaffold

- **Why it matters:** Tiny uppercase mono text above nearly every section creates visual noise and conflicts with Unit's own no-decoration rule.
- **Fix:** Keep only meaningful data/status labels. Let headings carry section hierarchy, and render navigation/footer labels as normal sentence-case text.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Narrative text is too small on several routes

- **Why it matters:** 15px body copy and 10–13px supporting text slows reading, especially on mobile and legal/support pages.
- **Fix:** Raise narrative copy to 16px, preserve smaller sizes only for true metadata, and align hero tracking/leading with the documented web type ramp.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

### [P3] Mobile trust and review sections waste space

- **Why it matters:** The trust band wraps around a stranded bullet and review cards reserve large empty areas, stretching the page without adding meaning.
- **Fix:** Stack the two trust statements on mobile and simplify reviews into divided editorial quotes without minimum card heights.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-time lifter):** The opening promise works, but ten equally weighted feature cards obscure which three capabilities matter first. Small support copy and repeated labels add reading cost before confidence is established.

**Alex (experienced program follower):** The concrete last-result and next-target screenshots are strong, but repeated marketing containers slow scanning. Program/compare pages should make their table and import steps readable without 13–15px text.

**Sam (privacy-conscious buyer):** Local storage and no-account claims are prominent and supported by legal routes. A missing hero photo or placeholder state can still signal an unfinished product and weaken the trust claim.

## Minor Observations

- Header navigation uses decorative mono uppercase styling where normal sentence-case labels scan faster.
- Footer category labels overuse the same eyebrow treatment.
- The audience strip is credible but can use a more orderly responsive grid.
- The updates hero repeats “What’s New” as both eyebrow and heading.

## Questions to Consider

- Which three product moments should a visitor remember if they only scroll halfway?
- Can Unit's calm notebook character come through with fewer containers and more open editorial rhythm?
- Does any section label add information that the heading does not already provide?
